## 1. Study Overview & Methodology
Before public dissemination, probabilistic risk communication must be tested against real citizens to ensure that probabilistic forecast intervals [$q_{10}, q_{90}$] and health advisories prompt protective public actions rather than fatalism or confusion.

* **Sample Size:** 6 participants (diverse socio-economic personas in Lahore).
* **Demographics:** University students, outdoor rickshaw drivers, primary school teachers/mothers of asthmatic children, construction site supervisors, retirees with heart conditions, and homemakers.
* **Testing Device:** Mobile smartphones (Android & iOS) over 3G/4G cellular connections.
* **Format:** 15-minute structured think-aloud interview with an initial 5-second look evaluating Questions 1 through 7.

## 3. Qualitative Question 6 Findings (Sharing & Suggestions)
* **High Viral Sharing Propensity:** All 6 participants stated they would share the card in their primary WhatsApp circles (family groups, rickshaw union channels, student campus groups, school parent chats).
* **Visual Clarity:** The prominent AQI color chip and short headline were cited as the primary reason the card was instantly readable during the 5-second first look.
* **Range Comprehension:** Participants remarked that presenting a "likely range" rather than a single fake number built credibility: *"Weather in Lahore is never one exact number; saying 8 days in 10 is honest."*
